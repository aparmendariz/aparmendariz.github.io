<p>
I am a PhD Candidate in Economics &amp; Econometrics at the University of St. Gallen, where I work at the Swiss Institute for Empirical Economic Research (SEW) under the supervision of Prof. Michael Lechner. I expect to complete my PhD in July 2027.
My research focuses on <b>causal inference, treatment effect estimation, and policy learning</b>, in both experimental and observational settings, with applications in <b>health economics</b>.
</p>

<p>
My job market paper, <a href="{{ site.url }}/assets/Armendariz_JMP.pdf" target="_blank"><i>Do Drug Consumption Rooms Reduce Drug-Related Hospitalizations? Evidence from Switzerland</i></a>, estimates the causal effect of drug consumption rooms on drug-related hospitalizations using a staggered difference-in-differences design on the universe of hospitalisations in Switzerland. See my <a href="{{ site.url }}/research">research page</a> for the full list of papers.
</p>

<p>
In spring 2026 I was a visiting PhD researcher at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, funded by an SNSF DocMobility Grant. Before my PhD, I completed a Master in Data Analytics and Economics at the University of Fribourg (<i>magna cum laude</i>) and a Bachelor in Economics at the Universidad de las Américas, Ecuador.
</p>

<p>
At SEW I contribute to <a href="https://mcfpy.github.io/mcf/">mcf</a>, an open-source Python package for Modified Causal Forests, and I am a teaching assistant for Causal Machine Learning (PhD level) and Microeconometrics (Master's level).
I bring expertise in <b>econometrics, machine learning, and data analysis</b>, with experience in Python, R, and SQL. I enjoy building tools that bridge econometric methods with practical policy applications.
</p>

<p>
Outside of academia, I enjoy learning languages and swimming. I speak Spanish, English, French, and Russian, and I am learning German. Since April 2026 I volunteer with Fundación Avanzar, designing and delivering workshops on costs and budgeting for women entrepreneurs from vulnerable backgrounds.
</p>
