<h2>Working papers</h2>

<ul class="papers">
	<li>
		<span class="paper-title">Do Drug Consumption Rooms Reduce Drug-Related Hospitalizations? Evidence from Switzerland</span>
		<div class="paper-meta"><span class="tag">Job Market Paper</span><span>Single author</span><a class="btn" href="{{ site.url }}/assets/Armendariz_JMP.pdf" target="_blank">PDF</a></div>
		<p class="paper-abstract">This paper estimates the causal effect of drug consumption room openings on drug-related hospitalizations in Switzerland, exploiting their staggered introduction across Swiss cities between 1998 and 2022 with individual-level hospital records and a difference-in-differences design. Openings reduce drug-related hospitalizations by roughly 35% within 5 km of a facility, with the largest reductions in fatal outcomes.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
		<span class="paper-title">Testing Effect Homogeneity and Confounding in High-Dimensional Experimental and Observational Studies</span>
		<div class="paper-meta"><span>With Martin Huber</span><a class="btn" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19703" target="_blank">arXiv</a></div>
		<p class="paper-abstract">We propose a framework for testing the homogeneity of conditional average treatment effects (CATEs) across multiple experimental and observational studies, using double machine learning to handle high-dimensional covariates.</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<h2>Work in progress</h2>

<ul class="papers">
	<li>
		<span class="paper-title">When Do Common Trends and Unconfoundedness Agree? An Overidentification Test Across Studies</span>
		<div class="paper-meta"><span>With Martin Huber</span></div>
		<p class="paper-abstract">Applied economists with panel data must often choose between difference-in-differences, which relies on parallel trends, and selection on observables, which relies on unconfoundedness. We use a Hausman-type overidentification test with machine-learning-based covariate control to assess whether the two strategies are jointly consistent, and apply it to the outcomes of 25 published DiD studies spanning labor, health, development, trade, and political economy.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
		<span class="paper-title">Precisely Zero: Migration's Null Effect on Crime</span>
		<div class="paper-meta"><span>With Riccardo Ciacci</span></div>
		<p class="paper-abstract">We examine the effect of migration on crime in Switzerland between 2009 and 2025, leveraging the quasi-random allocation of asylum seekers across cantons and detailed administrative data. We find precisely estimated null effects on overall, sex, violent, and property crime, human trafficking, and unauthorized prostitution, robust across migrant statuses, defendant nationalities, and crime specifications.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
		<span class="paper-title">Overfitting in Policy Learning</span>
		<div class="paper-meta"><span>With Michael Lechner, Jana Marečková &amp; Linda Odermatt</span></div>
		<p class="paper-abstract">We propose a method to detect overfitting in policy learning models and develop tools to help applied researchers mitigate this issue.</p>
	</li>
</ul>
